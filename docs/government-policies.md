# Government policy contract (T13 partial)

This document describes funded-transfer and food-subsidy behavior, scoped import tariffs and trade restrictions, local-purchase taxes, the explicit tax-event helper, and institutional command approval. It does not claim completion or acceptance of the broader T13 policy set or any SC scenario.

## Food subsidy: accounting and timing

Submit a `government.policy` command with `variant: "food-subsidy"`, the player `kingdomId`/`governmentId`, a configured recipient account and owner, a valid commodity, integer `amount`, and `effectiveTick`. `amount` is the total funded guarantee for the policy, not a per-unit amount. `expiryTick` is inclusive and configurable; when omitted the policy expires at `effectiveTick + 59`, covering exactly 60 ticks. A mapped institution's approval and delay rules still apply.

The command is approved only when the treasury can back the full guarantee at approval. For a given cohort account/owner, jurisdiction, and commodity, a second subsidy is rejected when its inclusive effective-tick window overlaps an existing subsidy; separate cohorts and non-overlapping windows are allowed. An empty/underfunded treasury records a `reject` decision with the available balance, required guarantee, and rationale in government state; no policy is installed. Subsidies are evaluated only alongside a real economy-settlement purchase for the configured beneficiary and commodity. The settlement credits that beneficiary from the government treasury using an accounting `payment`, within both remaining guarantee and available treasury. Purchase payment, commodity transfer, consumption, and subsidy payment settle together. No goods are created. If later funds are insufficient, the runtime records a `reduce` or `reject` decision with treasury, purchase, guarantee, rationale, and constraints. The guarantee is a bounded maximum payout and is virtually reserved against other government policy transfers while active; it is not an accounting escrow, so unrelated external debits can still reduce the eventual payout. Accounting journal entries are the record of cash movement.

The integration test exercises an unsubsidized baseline purchase, a real grain purchase subsidy, the 60-effective-tick inclusive expiry window, zero-treasury rejection, and conserved account balances. Subsidy does not pay on a tick without a corresponding purchase.

## Existing transfer, tax helper, and institution behavior

Legacy `government.policy` payloads without a variant continue to schedule funded transfers. Such transfers use accounting journal payments; insufficient balance does not make the treasury negative. `assessGovernmentTax` is an idempotent explicit taxable-event helper and only accepts payer/treasury accounts with matching ledger owners; caller-supplied kingdom IDs are not accepted as authorization. This helper is not the local-purchase tax path.

`decideInstitutionalPolicy` accepts bounded 0–100 legitimacy, quorum, and support inputs. When `GovernmentConfig.institutions` maps the player kingdom to an institution, the command requires the player/institution approval inputs, enforces thresholds/veto, records approval, and delays effectiveness. Omitted mappings preserve legacy behavior. Baselines are city council (legitimacy 50, support 40, quorum 60, veto, delay 2), monarchy (40, 30, no quorum/veto, delay 1), parliamentary (50, 50, quorum 60, veto, delay 3), and central republic (60, 50, no quorum, veto, delay 1). These are operational baselines, not a moral ranking, election model, or transition rule.

## Local-purchase tax

Submit `government.policy` with `variant: "local-purchase-tax"`, player government IDs, a unique policy ID, `rateBps` from 1 through 10,000, `incidence: "buyer" | "seller"`, and inclusive `effectiveTick`/`expiryTick`. A government cannot approve another local-purchase-tax policy whose inclusive tick window overlaps its existing tax policy; distinct governments and non-overlapping windows are allowed. Configured institutional approval is required and must allow the effective tick after delay. `GovernmentConfig.cityKingdomIds` is trusted jurisdiction configuration; caller payloads do not choose jurisdiction. At a real local-economy purchase, the buyer city (including live cohort relocation) determines its jurisdiction. Buyer-incidence tax participates in the affordability bound; seller-incidence tax is withheld from seller proceeds. Tax, seller payment, commodity transfer/consumption, and any applicable subsidy use one accounting transaction. Integer basis-point calculations truncate fractional minor units; all products use bigint and checked safe-integer conversion. Zero-tax behavior is unchanged when no matching active policy exists.

The integration test checks an actual purchase, treasury credit, conserved account balances, duplicate-charge prevention, policy expiry, and deterministic snapshot replay. A cross-jurisdiction fixture and a distinct seller-incidence fixture are not yet included.

## Scoped import tariff and restriction

`government.policy` accepts `import-tariff` and `trade-restriction` variants only when `importKingdomId` equals the configured player government's own kingdom ID. A foreign government cannot set another kingdom's import policy. Policies explicitly scope that import kingdom, route, commodity, inclusive effective tick, and inclusive expiry tick; tariffs also carry a positive integer amount. Overlapping tariffs deliberately stack: each matching policy is charged, with all tariff amounts included in the same atomic departure transaction. Overlapping trade restrictions remain harmlessly restrictive. When an institution is configured, the approval inputs and delay rule apply. At actual `logistics.depart`, the destination kingdom is taken from trusted logistics route/city mappings, and the import treasury from configured government mappings. The payer jurisdiction is derived from the trusted source-city mapping and the buyer account owner, never from payload jurisdiction strings. A matching tariff is debited from the buyer and credited to the import treasury in the same accounting transaction as freight and cargo reservation/departure. Any insufficient funds rejects the whole departure transaction. Matching restrictions reject only new departures; already-in-transit shipments are untouched, and expired policies no longer match. Existing diplomacy permit checks remain independent and unchanged.

## Funded city-service capital investment

Submit `government.policy` with `variant: "capital-investment"`, the player kingdom IDs, unique `policyId`, `targetCityId`, existing `service` key, `contractorAccountId`, positive integer `cost` (maximum 1,000,000), positive `capacityIncrease` (maximum 1,000), and inclusive `effectiveTick`/`expiryTick`. The target city must exist in the registered city-service state and its jurisdiction must match the trusted `GovernmentConfig.cityKingdomIds` entry. The contractor must be that city's configured `serviceProviderAccountId`; caller labels cannot select a different payee. The treasury must cover cost at command execution. The contract is paid immediately as one accounting journal payment, so rejection leaves both balances and investment state unchanged. Configured institutional approval and delay apply; the government decision records cause, alternatives, rationale, treasury/contractor/city/capacity facts, and constraints.

At the effective tick, the investment increments the actual `CityState.cities[cityId].serviceCapacity[service]` used by city-service allocation. This is an idempotent one-time increase bounded so total service capacity is at most 1,000; approval checks the maximum aggregate capacity across overlapping scheduled investments as well as capacity already applied, and rejects overcommitment before payment. It is removed once after the inclusive expiry tick. The same capacity therefore feeds service coverage and related city behavior, rather than a shadow metric. The integration test checks unchanged capacity before effectiveness, increased capacity while active, restoration at expiry, contractor/treasury journal transfer, and tick-bounded one-time application.

Road investment is not exposed: logistics route capacity is currently fixed by trusted configuration and checked as immutable configuration by its invariant; it has no supported mutable capital/throughput hook. Changing the configured route after registration or writing a separate throughput number would be fake state, so this implementation deliberately does neither.

## Unresolved scope / Q08

Q08 remains unresolved. This work does not supply an election/legislative/judicial detail or an institution-transition rule and therefore does **not** claim SC-11 transition success. Local-purchase tax is integrated with local cohort purchases only; logistics tariff behavior is separate. Tax policy currently applies to all commodities purchased in a mapped jurisdiction (no commodity/city sub-scope selector beyond the configured city-to-kingdom mapping). No broad SC-07/08/11/14/17 or T13 acceptance claim is made.
