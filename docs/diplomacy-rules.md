# Bilateral diplomacy and targeted trade rules (T14)

This slice extends the existing, explicitly accepted bilateral trade-permit flow. It is not a claim that SC-04/09/10/17 or the broader diplomacy feature is complete.

## Relations and decisions

Diplomacy config may provide one relation per kingdom pair with `trust` and named `interests`, each an integer from 0 through 100. Duplicate pairs are rejected regardless of ordering. Missing relations are initialized symmetrically as trust 50 and trade interest 50. Relation runtime state is bounds-checked by the invariant rather than frozen to its initial hash. Player decisions remain explicit commands and are never synthesized as AI actions. AI treaty decisions record alternatives, observable inputs, integer scores, constraints, cause, and reason. The score starts at 100 when both configured reserve thresholds pass (otherwise 0), then adjusts by trust and commodity interest relative to 50; acceptance requires 100. This preserves reserve-only defaults while explicit low trust can override adequate reserves and high trust/interest can support an offer despite low reserves. AI decision records are one per offer/kingdom. Explicit treaty decisions and autonomous treaty/sanction decisions update pair trust once, with each transition clamped to 0–100.

## Treaty terms and logistics

An existing bilateral offer may additionally specify `effectiveTick` and `expiryTick` (both required together, inclusive), a positive integer departure `quota`, a `tariffDiscountBps` value from 0 through 10,000, and a nonnegative integer `withdrawalPenalty` in currency minor units. Accepted decisions from both counterparties are required. The permit is usable only during its effect window. Existing offers without terms retain their prior unbounded permit behavior.

The quota counts departures assigned to that offer, not quantity, within the offer window. When several accepted offers overlap on one route and commodity, departures use the first eligible offer with remaining quota in configuration order; each shipment records its `treatyOfferId`. If every matching offer is exhausted, the departure is rejected. A matching treaty reduces each already-configured, matching government import tariff by the specified basis-point discount; it does not change the government policy or route fare. The integer tariff is rounded down using exact integer arithmetic. Freight and any remaining tariff are accounted as separate payments in the same accounting transaction as cargo reservation and departure. A zero remainder is not charged. Failure rejects the departure without committing any of those effects. Existing shipments are not reconsidered when terms expire.

## Scope and limitations

A player-controlled issuer may submit `diplomacy.sanction` with a unique sanction ID, a target kingdom, route, commodity, inclusive effective/expiry ticks, and a nonempty reason. It blocks only new departures on that route and commodity when the route crosses the issuer/target pair; expiry restores eligibility, and already-paid in-transit cargo is untouched. An AI target independently records a deterministic `respond`/`abstain` decision for a player-issued sanction, including stable decision ID, alternatives, trust/interest inputs, scores, constraints, and reason. Response requires the bounded score threshold and a configured route in the reverse city-to-kingdom direction. It creates only a targeted counter-sanction on that selected route for the observed commodity/window; counter-sanctions do not trigger further decisions, and no player decision is fabricated. A party to an accepted treaty may submit `diplomacy.withdrawal` with its offer ID, player kingdom ID, future effective tick, and nonempty reason. The withdrawal takes effect inclusively at that tick, stops the treaty permit and its tariff/quota terms, and never cancels already-paid cargo. One withdrawal is allowed per treaty. When the withdrawal is early (before treaty expiry, or for an unbounded treaty), its configured positive penalty is paid immediately from the withdrawing kingdom's treasury to the other party's treasury in one accounting transaction; insufficient funds or missing/unambiguous treasury accounts rejects the command without changing diplomacy or accounting. Zero/omitted penalties do not create a payment. Treasury accounts use registered government treasury IDs when available; otherwise a uniquely named `<kingdomId>-treasury` or sole account owned by that kingdom is required. AI/player withdrawals are rejected; no AI command is accepted. The existing government import-tariff and trade-restriction policies remain separate and retain their issuer/import-jurisdiction rules. Retaliation is never automatic: only the target AI's independently recorded `respond` choice creates a counter-sanction; `abstain` records no sanction. No multilateral support, scenario-wide acceptance, or full institution/election model is claimed.
